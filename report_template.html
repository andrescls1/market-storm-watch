Causal crash-risk model · version 1 · data through Aug 2026

Market Storm Watch

A first working version of the causal early-warning model: a theory-built causal graph, tested with PCMCI across three eras, feeding a walk-forward "watch" model for the chance of a 20%+ fall in the S&P 500 within 24 months. Built on 155 years of Shiller data. The credit and stress layers come in Step 2.

What version 1 found

Timing: no skill

Out of sample (1925–2023), no version of the multivariate model beat the plain historical base rate. It raised a watch before 1929, 1968, 1973, 2000, 2007 and 2021, but also through the calm 1950s and 2010s.

Valuation: weak signal

CAPE on its own ranked risk best (AUC 0.61, where 0.5 is a coin flip), but its probabilities were still no better calibrated than the base rate.

Stable mechanism

Inflation leading to rising yields held in every era. Most links into market declines appeared in one era only, which is exactly the regime-dependence problem the book warns about.

Minsky shows up

Since 1990, long calm stretches were followed by deeper drawdowns about 9 months later, the only era where the "stability breeds instability" link appeared.

Scorecard

All models scored on the same 977 months (1925–2023, not already in a bear market). AUC measures ranking: 0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 is perfect. Brier skill compares the probabilities with the base rate: above zero is better, below zero is worse.

Robustness: simpler and more regularized versions

All variants are reported, not just the best one, because picking a winner after seeing the test results would overfit. None has a positive Brier skill.

The causal graph, tested

The theory graph was drawn from economic mechanisms first, then checked with PCMCI (a causal discovery algorithm for time series) on quarterly data at lags of up to two years. A cell shows the strongest lagged partial correlation found, with its lag in quarters. ✓ means the sign matches theory, ✗ means it is reversed, and a dash means no significant link (p < 0.01).

The roadmap

1

Baseline graph and watch model (this page)

Valuation, rates, earnings, momentum, volatility and Minsky layers, with walk-forward scoring.

2

Credit and stress layers

Add credit growth, spreads and the yield curve, and re-test the graph. The watch model is expected to improve most here, since credit booms have the strongest record in the research.

3

Multi-country panel

Train on 18 countries so the model learns from dozens of crises, and test which links are invariant across countries.

4

Regimes and the warning model

Add a latent sentiment factor, regime switching, and a short-horizon warning model built on spreads and volatility.

5

Interventions

Fit the structural equations and ask do() questions, such as the effect on crash risk of a 100 bp rate hike.

Data: Robert Shiller monthly S&P 500 dataset (via github.com/datasets/s-and-p-500), CPI-U and 10-year Treasury yields (github.com/datasets). Method follows the structure of Fabozzi & Focardi, Causal Modeling for Finance and Business (MIT Press). Monthly-average prices soften sharp drops: the 2020 fall reads as about 19% and does not count as a bear market here. Research tool, not investment advice.
